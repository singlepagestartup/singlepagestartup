# AI Chat document guidance

These explanations apply Studio's document templates and role methods to AI Chat. The section names and ownership rules are Studio conventions. External references explain the underlying methods; they do not prescribe this exact form or establish results for a particular business. Sources were checked on 2026-10-07. Project observations and customer evidence belong in the project documents, with their own attribution.

<!-- document: brief -->

# Brief.md

Brief records the owner's business, products, customers, current resources and intended outcomes. Account Manager keeps it compact and preserves exact names, requirements and source attribution. Strategy, Brand, Design and each Product use this starting point to check their scope and constraints.

## How to use it

Record what is known, who supplied it and what remains uncertain. Separate an existing capability, an owner's intention, a customer claim and a measured result. A goal is something you want to reach; a constraint is a limit the work must respect. Resolve contradictions in chat before treating a statement as confirmed.

Visual reference intake records supplied identity and taste, with the actual files. A liked image establishes a preference; it does not select the final design or establish permission to reuse the image.

## Basis

Studio's six-section Brief is a compact intake format. The [SBA's business-planning guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) covers customers, value, operations and finances in lean plans. Studio uses those concerns at intake and elaborates the model in each Product. A completed Brief records reviewed inputs; customer demand still needs evidence.

<!-- section: Project and products -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check which business and offers later decisions concern. A reference project, a marketing channel and a product may have different purposes even when they share an owner or technology.

## What to record

Give the project name, purpose and boundary. List confirmed products with stable IDs, exact names, owners and current or intended availability. Identify supporting examples and explicit exclusions. Keep drafts in scope when the owner has confirmed them.

## Review boundary

Confirm scope with the owner. Product priority belongs in Strategy; a possible future revenue activity does not become a confirmed product automatically.

<!-- section: Customers and value -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to connect the offer to the person or organization that receives value. The person using an offer may differ from its buyer or payer.

## What to record

Describe known customers, their situation and problem, and the benefit currently delivered or intended. Distinguish user, buyer and beneficiary where material. Attribute customer statements and identify inferred needs.

## Review boundary

An owner's belief about demand remains a claim. Customer research and the Product's value proposition can examine it without turning it into a measured finding.

<!-- section: Current state -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check what the business can support today before planning or making public promises.

## What to record

Describe existing work, tested capabilities, observed results and available assets. Give the source, date or observation window when it changes the meaning. Supplied notes here are intake to review and summarize, not automatically verified facts.

## Review boundary

Separate internal tests from customer adoption, and planned capabilities from working ones. Leave conflicting or uninspected material attributed until it is checked.

<!-- section: Business and resources -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check whether later offers and promises fit the owner's delivery, money and capacity constraints.

## What to record

Capture known channels, delivery, payer, price and payment unit, revenue, funding, people, partners, costs, capacity and decision authority. Include currency, period, scope and source for numbers. Identify rights or dependencies that limit use.

## Review boundary

Keep unsupplied prices, budgets and commitments unknown. Each Product elaborates its economics; Brief retains the facts needed to understand the business independently.

<!-- section: Goals and success -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to judge strategic choices against the owner's intended outcome. More traffic, paid use and customer progress can require different decisions.

## What to record

State desired outcomes and priorities. Include a baseline, target, time horizon and owner when supplied. Explain which observable change would count as progress.

## Review boundary

Keep intentions distinct from results. Give numerical targets a source or calculation basis; leave an unsupplied target open rather than inventing one.

<!-- section: Visual reference intake -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to establish the client's taste and existing identity before Design selects a visual system.

## What to record

Keep supplied identity separate from five reference categories: interface and website, typography, photography, illustration and marketing creative. Attach exact files and record liked or disliked traits, comments, provenance, rights and permitted use. Identify each file by name or asset ID.

## Review boundary

Distinguish confirmed absence, material not supplied and a category explicitly outside scope. An unclassified upload stays open. Studio's photography intake uses at least four references to examine recurring traits; this is an internal review rule, not a statistical sample of customer preference.

# Strategy.md

Strategy describes the intended marketing system that fulfills the reviewed Brief: whom to serve, how products contribute, why customers should choose them, how channels work together and how outcomes guide priorities. Strategist owns these decisions.

## Concepts and use

Positioning connects real customer alternatives to differentiated capabilities, value, a suitable audience and market context. Product roles describe how each confirmed offer contributes to the project. A growth system connects channels and the customer experience to acquisition, adoption, continued use or recommendations.

Write a coherent target state and durable decision rules. Detailed offers, calendars and campaign budgets stay with their product. Keep operator-controlled facts such as spending and support capacity attributed; external research cannot supply them.

## Basis

Studio's five sections combine these concerns. [April Dunford's positioning method](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning) starts from customers' actual alternatives. [Jobs to Be Done](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/) examines progress in particular circumstances. [NN/g's journey-mapping guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/journey-mapping-101/) keeps the actor, scenario and experience explicit. These are reasoning methods; the project's claims still need their own evidence.

<!-- section: Strategic direction -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to judge whether audiences, offers, channels and resource choices add up to the same intended business outcome.

## What to record

Describe the final marketing system, positioning and enduring trade-offs. Connect them to the reviewed Brief's goals and limits. State which alternatives customers actually consider and why the chosen direction fits the available value and proof.

## Review boundary

Keep the strategy focused on the target state. Preparation tasks and implementation roadmaps belong in their own work. Explain a material evidence gap beside the decision it affects.

<!-- section: Audiences and product roles -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check who each product serves and how its role contributes to the whole project.

## What to record

For each priority audience describe its situation, desired value and intended behavior. Map confirmed product IDs to those needs and to roles such as earning revenue, delivering value or supporting adoption of another offer.

## Review boundary

Keep customer evidence separate from proposed segmentation. Recognition and adoption differ from paid use. A lower priority does not remove a confirmed product from the catalog.

<!-- section: Growth system -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check how a person moves from discovering value to using, paying for or recommending the offer.

## What to record

Prioritize coordinated channels. For each name its audience, role, content or value, destination and contribution to growth. Explain how the channels and product experience reinforce one another within known resource limits.

## Review boundary

A platform list does not explain the mechanism. Label proposed channels separately from operating accounts and observed reach. Detailed publishing schedules and budgets belong to product Promotion.

<!-- section: Customer journey -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check whether the intended experience carries a particular customer from a relevant situation to useful value and continued use.

## What to record

Describe discovery, consideration, purchase or adoption, value received, help, continued use and recommendations where relevant. Keep the actor's goal and questions visible. Identify separate conversions when one service also demonstrates another product.

## Review boundary

Label a designed future journey as intended. Observed behavior needs customer data. Each Product's Sales elaborates the process; engineering steps are outside this journey.

<!-- section: Measurement and priorities -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to connect business outcomes to decisions about attention and resources.

## What to record

Pair each outcome with an observable signal and the management decision it informs. Distinguish recognition, activation, adoption, retention and revenue. State resource priorities and risks that could change the direction.

## Review boundary

Give targets a basis. Followers and interest alone do not establish revenue or adoption. Keep forecasts separate from observations and detailed experiments in the owning product work.

# Brand.md

Brand defines what the business means, promises and sounds like. It applies the reviewed Strategy to public identity, intended perception, message order, language and consistency across products. Communication Strategist defines the messages and language; Brand Designer completes the meaning before translating it visually in Design.

## Concepts and use

A promise tells an audience what to expect; proof sets the limit of what can be claimed. Message hierarchy orders situation, promise, supporting evidence, objections and the next action. Voice makes those meanings recognizable in actual sentences and terms.

Keep claims within current capabilities and evidence. Product names and commercial or support wording must retain their exact scope. Colors, fonts, logo drawing and imagery belong in Design.

## Basis

These five sections and their claim boundaries are Studio's brand method. The [government content-design framework](https://understand-digital-data-roles-skills.service.gov.uk/role/content-designer/) describes evidence-based, accessible content around user needs. The [content-strategy framework](https://understand-digital-data-roles-skills.service.gov.uk/role/content-strategist/) covers governance and content structure. They support the discipline of consistent, usable content, not a guarantee of brand preference or sales.

<!-- section: Brand identity -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check which business or offer a public name identifies and how product names relate to it.

## What to record

Give the exact brand name, product names and their relationship, naming and usage rules, and a slogan if one is approved. Preserve mandatory wording and clarify ambiguous names.

## Review boundary

Keep visual lockups and logo assets in Design's Identity application. A working name stays proposed until the owner confirms it.

<!-- section: Intended perception -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to judge whether messages express the character and difference the intended audience should understand.

## What to record

State what people should understand, trust and remember. Describe the intended character and relevant distinction, including impressions the brand must avoid.

## Review boundary

Intended perception is a design goal. Claims about what customers actually think need research. Avoid adjectives that have no consequence for wording or decisions.

<!-- section: Meaning and message hierarchy -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to decide what people need to understand before taking the next action.

## What to record

Connect the audience's situation to the promise, supporting evidence, likely objections and truthful responses. Order the messages and define a next action that fits the offer's current availability.

## Review boundary

Distinguish supplied customer objections from professional proposals. Each factual claim needs suitable proof and scope; a desired result does not establish a guarantee.

<!-- section: Voice and language -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to make writers' choices consistent in the terms, explanations and promises customers read.

## What to record

Define vocabulary, tone, sentence and evidence rules, prohibited claims and exact terminology. Add suitable and unsuitable wording examples for the actual offer and audience.

## Review boundary

Preserve binding names and commercial meaning while simplifying language. A generic tone adjective is insufficient without examples that show how it changes the text.

<!-- section: Consistency rules -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check that different products and channels describe the same business commitments.

## What to record

State rules for names, promises, evidence, money terms, support and domain-specific limits. Identify which rules apply across the project and which commercial conditions belong to one Product.

## Review boundary

Check public wording against the owning document's current terms. Keep rights, support or outcome promises within their confirmed scope. Product-specific campaign formats belong in Promotion.

# Design.md

Design turns reviewed brand meaning and client preferences into a reusable visual system: identity, color, typography, icons, interface rules, photography and illustration. Brand Designer owns the system and its assets. Product pages and campaigns apply it within their own scope.

## Concepts and use

Semantic colors name roles such as text, surface or action so usage stays coherent. A visual preference profile records observable traits from reviewed references, separately from the final design choices. Provenance connects an output to its originals, prompt, tool, rights and review state.

Photography and illustration each need purpose, evidence limits, a style prompt, production specification, actual example assets and a review gate. Studio preserves the original master and prepares a square delivery separately by expanding the canvas; it does not stretch or discard the original.

## Basis

Studio owns this eight-section visual method and its production rules. The [interaction-design framework](https://understand-digital-data-roles-skills.service.gov.uk/role/interaction-designer/) covers service flow and interaction decisions. [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) defines accessibility criteria, including text alternatives, keyboard operation, contrast and focus. Written rules require checks in the actual interface; a document alone does not establish accessibility conformance.

<!-- section: Design intent -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to judge visual choices against reviewed client taste and the brand's intended character.

## What to record

Describe the client visual preference profile for interface and website, typography, photography, illustration and marketing creative. Cite actual reference IDs and distinguish explicit requests from inferred shared traits. State brand character, reusable graphic language and practical do/do-not rules.

## Review boundary

Resolve conflicts and obtain confirmation of inferred preferences. One reviewed category does not approve another. Final colors, typefaces and generated images follow the reviewed direction.

<!-- section: Identity application -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check that the approved name and identity remain recognizable and usable in different contexts.

## What to record

Define naming and lockups, spacing, minimum size, placement and variants for the surfaces that need them. Attach actual identity sources and record their rights and permitted use.

## Review boundary

Preserve Brand's naming decisions. Label a proposed identity separately from approved files, and test variants where they will appear.

<!-- section: Visual system -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to make color and type choices reusable across products and readable in their intended contexts.

## What to record

Define semantic color roles, light and dark usage where needed, and typography families, real weights, languages, sources, licenses, fallbacks and line height. State the role of each selection and show examples when choosing among options.

## Review boundary

Check the actual color combinations and text against applicable [WCAG contrast criteria](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum). A token name or a palette alone does not establish readable usage.

<!-- section: Iconography -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to keep actions, navigation and statuses understandable within one coherent icon family.

## What to record

Name the library, version, weight, source and license, or the custom drawing method and editable sources. Specify grid, stroke or fill, optical alignment, sizes, colors and states. Attach actual glyph examples and state their accessible labels.

## Review boundary

Check what each icon communicates in context. [WCAG text-alternative rules](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-content) cover meaningful non-text content; decorative icons require different treatment. A card naming an icon is not a glyph specimen.

<!-- section: Interface and product surfaces -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check whether reusable controls and compositions support the intended user actions across products.

## What to record

Define layout, hierarchy, spacing and component behavior, including default, selected, disabled, pending, empty, error and success states. Address responsiveness, keyboard use, focus, contrast, touch targets and reduced motion where relevant.

## Review boundary

Check the implemented experience against applicable [WCAG criteria](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/). Keep each product's pages, journey and final copy in its own work. References describe patterns, not a working product capability.

<!-- section: Photography -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to define what photographs communicate and which recurring traits make them belong to the same visual system.

## What to record

Include purpose and evidence boundary, a compact style master prompt, production specification, generation examples with exact asset IDs, and a review gate. Describe recurring light, color, texture and movement; individual scene briefs own subjects and props.

## Review boundary

Preserve the unconstrained master and create a separate square delivery through canvas expansion. Review both. Record tools, prompts, rights and permitted use. Illustrative scenes must not masquerade as customer evidence or real results.

<!-- section: Illustration and diagrams -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to choose the abstraction that explains an idea without implying unsupported business facts.

## What to record

Include purpose and evidence boundary, a style master prompt, production specification, exact example asset IDs and a review gate. Define recurring line, shape, color and motion traits. Scene or relationship briefs state what each illustration or diagram must explain.

## Review boundary

Keep generated scenes separate from evidence, and actual diagrams traceable to their inputs. Preserve masters and prepare square deliveries separately where needed. Review clarity, visual consistency, rights and permitted use.

<!-- section: Outputs and provenance -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check which files are usable, where they came from and which version applies to a product.

## What to record

List actual supplied and generated asset IDs, originals and delivery files, origin, prompt, tool, rights, usage limits and review state. Keep references separate from generated outputs. Attach inspectable files to their owning sections.

## Review boundary

A filename or planned prompt does not establish a completed asset. Approval concerns the inspected file and its recorded use. A later replacement requires reviewing affected uses; the original remains traceable.

# Products.md

Products is the project's catalog. Business Analyst retains every confirmed product with a stable identity and its own document. This includes drafts and offers outside the current strategic priority.

## How to use it

List product IDs, names and concise summaries. Create a product document for each confirmed offer. Each Product owns its customer, value, offer and economics; Sales, Promotion, Analytics and Research keep their own related work. Strategic priority belongs in Strategy's Measurement and priorities.

Shared resources are recorded in each consuming Product with its allocation basis. One shared owner or technology does not create another product automatically.

## Basis

This catalog follows Studio's product ownership and catalog rules. The [Business Model Canvas](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas) describes a connected business model; Studio uses its concerns within product work, without requiring a duplicate canvas or a combined portfolio model. A catalog entry records scope, not proof of demand.

<!-- section: Products -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check that every confirmed offer has a stable identity and a place for its decisions and evidence.

## What to record

List the confirmed ID, exact name, concise summary and owning document for each product. Keep draft status and current or intended availability explicit. Retain products outside the current priority.

## Review boundary

Confirm new products with the owner. Supporting examples and channels remain context unless they are separate offers. Detailed customer, offer, money and resource decisions belong in each Product.

# Product document

Each Product describes one offer's customers, problem, value, intended experience, operating resources and economics. Business Analyst maintains attributed intake; Strategist forms the offer within the reviewed Strategy. The first twelve sections follow Studio's Product template. Sales, Promotion and Analytics & Research retain related product work in this editor.

## Concepts and use

Customer segments distinguish the user, buyer, payer and other decision roles. Desired progress describes what a customer is trying to accomplish in a particular situation. A value proposition connects an offer's capabilities to that progress. Revenue, costs and funding describe different money flows; a free offer still consumes resources and may serve an adoption goal.

Keep client facts, calculations, intentions and hypotheses distinct. State an unknown beside the decision it affects. Shared resources carry this Product's share and allocation basis so the same expense is not counted twice.

## Basis

Studio adapts the [Business Model Canvas](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas) and [official block definitions](https://www.strategyzer.com/business-models-the-toolkit-to-design-a-disruptive-company) to product work. [Jobs to Be Done](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/) informs circumstance and progress; the [Value Proposition Canvas](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-value-proposition-canvas) connects customer needs to the offer. A modeled fit is a hypothesis until customer evidence supports it. Filling these sections does not establish market fit or profitability.

<!-- section: Product identity -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check which offer the customer, resource and money decisions describe.

## What to record

State the brand, distinct product name, one-sentence category, current foundation, owner and intended development direction. Define what belongs to the product and which related offers or showcases are outside its boundary.

## Review boundary

Preserve Brief's confirmed product identity and Brand's naming rules. Keep intended development distinct from current availability; approval records the reviewed version.

<!-- section: Customer Segments -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check whose problem, purchasing decision and value the offer addresses.

## What to record

Give each distinct segment a stable ID. Distinguish user, buyer, payer, beneficiary and decision-maker where they differ. State the qualifying situation, trigger, current alternatives, relevant characteristics and exclusions. Sales uses the same segment IDs.

## Review boundary

Attribute supplied customers and observed behavior. Label proposed segments as hypotheses until evidence supports them. Do not merge roles merely because the same organization is involved.

<!-- section: Problem and desired progress -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to examine the customer outcome behind a request for features.

## What to record

Describe the circumstance and trigger, progress sought, functional constraints and material social or emotional forces. State pains, gains and how the customer recognizes progress.

## Review boundary

[Jobs to Be Done](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/) examines decisions through circumstance and desired progress. A proposed motive is reasoning to investigate; an interview finding needs the actual source. Keep the customer outcome separate from the implementation solution.

<!-- section: Value Propositions -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to explain why this offer could be preferable to the customer's actual alternatives.

## What to record

Compare the status quo and relevant alternatives. Connect differentiated capabilities to practical customer value, then to the segment's jobs, pains and gains. State which capabilities exist and which are proposed.

## Review boundary

The [Value Proposition Canvas](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-value-proposition-canvas) maps customer needs and offered value. That mapping requires customer evidence before a claim of fit. Keep unsupported superiority or outcome claims proposed.

<!-- section: Offer and usage -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check what the customer can obtain, what it includes and how useful value is reached.

## What to record

Define access or deliverable unit, included and excluded scope, intended use, continuation, support, rights and dependencies. Describe the experience at the customer's level.

## Review boundary

Keep money terms in Revenue Streams and the whole customer process in Sales. Support and rights promises need an owner and confirmed scope. Product requirements here concern the offer rather than installation or debugging.

<!-- section: Revenue Streams -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check who pays for which unit and how money reaches the business.

## What to record

For each segment state payer, unit, price or pricing rule, currency, one-off or recurring cadence, payment timing and conditions. For a free offer describe the intended non-cash exchange or adoption contribution, separately from cash revenue.

## Review boundary

Funding and subsidies are separate from customer revenue. Sales references these terms rather than inventing another price. Give prices and forecasts a source or basis; an intended exchange does not establish observed revenue.

<!-- section: Key Activities -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check what work must happen for the offer to deliver its stated value.

## What to record

Describe the activities that create and deliver value and the owner of each. Connect them to the resources, partners and costs they require. Reference Sales for the customer process.

## Review boundary

Distinguish an operating activity from a proposed one. Do not assume an owner has time, authority or capacity without input. Keep engineering task lists in engineering work.

<!-- section: Key Resources -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check that the offer's delivery and support commitments have the required resources.

## What to record

Identify people, infrastructure, finance and intellectual resources with stable IDs, owners, availability and constraints. For a shared resource record this Product's share and the basis for the allocation.

## Review boundary

Access to a tool does not establish staff capacity or rights. Each consuming Product records its share consistently; referencing a resource does not create another budget.

<!-- section: Key Partnerships -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check dependencies on people or organizations outside the product owner.

## What to record

Name suppliers and partners, their contribution, the dependency's significance, commercial constraints and supporting evidence. Keep unresolved commitments explicit.

## Review boundary

An integration, public API or desired relationship does not establish a partnership agreement. Separate a confirmed arrangement from a proposal and examine the decision affected by its absence.

<!-- section: Cost Structure -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check what delivering the offer costs and how that cost relates to resources, prices and available capital.

## What to record

Describe fixed and variable costs, known amounts, currency, period and calculation assumptions. Separate cash expense from founder time. State each shared allocation and its basis. Under Funding record who supplies capital or subsidy, conditions and limits, separately from Revenue Streams.

## Review boundary

Keep the same expense from being counted in full across multiple products. Budgets, capital and margins require inputs or an explicit calculation. Unsupplied money commitments stay unknown.

<!-- section: Assumptions and decision rules -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to identify which uncertainties can change the offer or its economics.

## What to record

Give material assumptions stable IDs, sources and states. Connect each unknown, objection or signal to the decision and its consequence. Link the Research that examines it, with its limits.

## Review boundary

An owner's claim, a professional hypothesis and a measured finding have different evidential status. Unknowns constrain the decisions that need them; unrelated missing details need not block attributed intake.

<!-- section: Business goals and metrics -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to judge the product against its intended contribution to the business and customer.

## What to record

Select applicable acquisition, value, adoption, revenue, retention or recommendation goals. Pair each with a metric, source or observation window and the management decision it informs. State the basis for numerical targets.

## Review boundary

Keep intended outcomes separate from measured results. A popular offer and a profitable one can have different signals. Runtime acceptance checks belong to engineering; business learning examines commercial assumptions.

<!-- section: Sales -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check how each customer segment discovers, chooses, receives and continues using the offer.

## What to record

Use the Product's stable segment IDs. Describe roles, trigger, criteria, objections, truthful responses and acquisition context. Map the entire customer process with actions, questions, owner, entry and exit, alternatives, help and continuation. Reference the Product's money terms.

## Review boundary

[NN/g's journey mapping](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/journey-mapping-101/) distinguishes actor, scenario and experience. Label future processes as intended. A free offer has an adoption decision; do not invent a purchase.

<!-- section: Promotion -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to check how this particular offer is presented publicly within the project's reviewed meaning and visual system.

## What to record

Describe the relevant Website, Marketing Creative and Presentation. Connect each surface to its audience, purpose, supported messages, next action and actual assets. Keep product-specific formats and copy here.

## Review boundary

Apply the reviewed Strategy, Brand and Design without redefining them. Keep a planned surface separate from a working one. Recheck public claims against the Product's current capabilities, terms and proof.

<!-- section: Analytics & Research -->

## Why this section exists

Use it to examine the Product's assumptions with observations and external findings.

## What to record

For Analytics give the source, definition and observation window of each measure. For Research state the decision question, method, scope, exact sources, findings and limits. Connect findings to stable assumption IDs.

## Review boundary

Keep measured results separate from interpretation and hypotheses. External research cannot supply the owner's price, capacity or authority. A reported association alone does not establish what caused a result in this business.
