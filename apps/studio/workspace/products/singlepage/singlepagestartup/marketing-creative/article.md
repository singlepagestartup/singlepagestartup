# Add your business rules without rebuilding the basics.

Your product begins with something a customer needs. A useful order rule, a way to share information or a service that fits how their business works. The foundation should give you room to develop that idea.

![People exchanging ideas at a worktable.](/workspace-assets/singlepage/generated/measured-space/singlepagestartup-photography-business-conversation.png)

## Start with the business, then shape the product

Before asking an agent to create screens, describe the business. Who is the customer? What do they need? What will they receive, and how will the business earn money?

Those answers give the agent a direction. The product can then be organized around a customer journey instead of a collection of disconnected features.

You do not need to describe the code. Explain the behavior and the result you want. Claude Code or Codex Desktop can work with the project while you make decisions about the product.

## Reuse what your product needs

Code Framework provides a shared frontend and backend, documentation and instructions for coding agents. Common functions include accounts and access, catalogs, carts, orders and payment integrations.

| Start with                 | Concentrate your work on                                             |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Registration and access    | Who your customers are and what they can do.                         |
| Catalogs, carts and orders | What you sell and the rules of your buying experience.               |
| Payment integrations       | The commercial offer and the providers appropriate for your project. |
| Project instructions       | The business decisions and changes the agent should work from.       |

The aim is to put more of your development effort and AI tokens into the logic that distinguishes your product. Choose the functions that fit; the framework does not determine your business model for you.

## Describe one meaningful change

Imagine a store where customers order at least six units at a time. The maker's request can be simple:

> My store has a minimum order of six units. Add that rule to the existing cart. Keep the current account and payment flow, and show me how the ordering experience changes.

The business rule is the point of the request. The agent adapts the product; you explore whether the result expresses your idea and serves the customer. Continue the conversation as the idea becomes clearer.

This is an example of a request, not a rule every SPS project must use. A different business might need a booking policy, a member role or a new step in an order journey.

## The chat is another way to discover the foundation

AI Chat is the planned hosted service for answers grounded in a user's business facts and documents. It also introduces the foundation to people who want to create their own product.

Someone can keep using the hosted chat and buy tokens. An interested maker can follow the local-project route to GitHub. Obtaining Code Framework does not require a chat-token purchase.

## Make it yours, then use it again

Describe your business, choose the common functions it needs and work with a coding agent on your own rules, content and design. Keep improving that product. When another idea fits the foundation, use it again.

The framework code and documentation are free. Coding-agent services, infrastructure and chosen external services are separate expenses. Help and maintenance are provided as time permits.
